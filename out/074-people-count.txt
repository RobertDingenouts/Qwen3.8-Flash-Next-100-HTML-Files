TITLE: People Count — one hundred humans, one election
PROMPT: A field guide to proportional representation as a living isotype: one hundred hand-drawn citizens re-paint themselves the instant you nudge the electorate, and a dry one-liner tells you who is governing and who is about to suffer coalition conversations.
DESCRIPTION: A terracotta civic poster with a 10×10 parliament of tiny canvas people — each drawn as a head circle over a quadratic-curve torso — coloured by party block in civic red, labour teal, greens and independents. Two sliders steer the left bloc and greens shares (the remainder splits between the others), SNAP ELECTION rolls a completely random electorate, and seats allocate by proper largest-remainder apportionment so the hundred always sum to a hundred. Clicking a party legend strikes it out of parliament and the whole house re-seats around the vacancy, with the footer reporting the largest party and whether it can govern alone.
TECHNIQUES: largest-remainder seat apportionment with live renormalisation, isotype grid rendering of composite canvas figures (arc heads + quadratic torsos), party-strikeout state machine with full re-allocation, dual-slider constrained share editing, random electorate sampling.
INTERACTION: Slide the LEFT BLOC and GREENS dials to watch the people change shirts seat by seat, SNAP ELECTION for a surprise parliament, strike parties from the legend to simulate boycotts, and read the governing verdict at the bottom.
