TITLE: Paper Wing — press, drag back, release
PROMPT: A schoolyard physics toy drawn like a picture book: a paper airplane with a flap-wobbling fuselage that you hurl with a slingshot gesture, gliding over a parallax sky past hand-marked distance stakes into a rolling green field.
DESCRIPTION: A daytime canvas: banded blue sky, drifting four-circle clouds on slow parallax, a bumpy grass horizon, and grey distance stakes every fifty metres with procedurally seeded fir trees. Press anywhere and drag backwards — a dashed red rubber-band shows the vector while the plane hovers at a fifth of the pull — release and the paper dart launches with speed-scaled lift fighting a gentle gravity and quadratic drag, its nose rotating toward the velocity vector and its tail wobbling with a flap sine. The camera chases once you’re airborne; landing computes metres (pixels/40), updates BEST, and a fresh press re-winds the next flight.
TECHNIQUES: slingshot input with clamped vector and proportional pre-launch displacement, gliding flight model (speed-dependent lift opposing gravity with drag), velocity-tracked nose attitude with smoothed rotation, camera follow with clamped scroll, seeded roadside furniture and parallax cloud field, localStorage personal best.
INTERACTION: Press the sky, drag back like a paper catapult, RELEASE to throw — glide angle is yours to learn — and chase the BEST line in the corner across sessions.
