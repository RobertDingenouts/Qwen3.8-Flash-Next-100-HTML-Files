TITLE: Keystroke — thirty seconds, honest fingers
PROMPT: A typing test stripped to its sermon: one quiet paper page of words, a blinking caret under the next word, instant red for lies, and a crimson sparkline sketching the rise or collapse of your WPM in real time.
DESCRIPTION: Forty-eight random words from a crafted pangram-ish pool sit on warm paper; you type into an invisible input (so the caret never deserts the prose) and each completed word turns black — or red with a bruise of background if it wasn’t the word on screen. WPM (gross, words-per-five-chars), accuracy, and the countdown headline the page; a thin sparkline underneath plots WPM every quarter-second. The finish line delivers one of four unsparing verdicts.
TECHNIQUES: hidden-input typing capture preserving visual caret state, per-word ok/bad class painting with deferred scoring on space, timer-driven WPM sampling into a rolling sparkline canvas, DPR-aware line chart, focus-recovery on board clicks, clean state reset.
INTERACTION: Just start typing — the clock begins at your first keystroke; BACKSPACE edits mid-word, wrong words flash red but keep your rhythm, RESTART ⌫ reshuffles the page, and thirty seconds later the page tells you exactly what it thinks.
