TITLE: Kepler Weather — telemetry from a planet with better ideas
PROMPT: A NASA-legacy mission-control dashboard for a world nobody will visit: temperature traces, a wind rose that actually points, a pressure gauge that sweats mbar, methane-rain odds, and twin moons crawling their arcs — all faked with total sincerity.
DESCRIPTION: Cream cards with hard black borders and offset shadows — pure retro-space infographic: a twenty-four-hour temperature spline with a dashed local-time cursor and floating readout, a compass rose whose orange needle reports heading and gusts, a semicircular pressure gauge with a gradient arc and live needle, a methane-rain probability slab, twin moonrise arcs on a horizon line, and an advisory that rotates with the sol number. The mission clock counts fractional sols; everything re-seeds on REFRESH TELEMETRY and drifts on its own every thirty seconds.
TECHNIQUES: seeded PRNG telemetry blocks keyed to sol+hour, sinusoidal diurnal temperature model with noise harmonics, canvas compass/gauge/spline drawing with DPR scaling, gradient-stroked gauge arcs, pseudo-moonrise phase geometry, advisory rotation by sol modulo.
INTERACTION: REFRESH TELEMETRY re-reads the instruments (the planet shrugs and rerolls slightly), resize re-fits every card, and the dashed cursor walks across the temperature trace as your own local time keeps the fiction honest.
