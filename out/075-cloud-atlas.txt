TITLE: A Cloud Atlas — ten species of the sky, zero image files
PROMPT: A naturalist’s field guide to clouds where every illustration is pure CSS: divs as puffs, border-radius as meteorology, and field-notes prose sardonic enough to make readers glance out the window.
DESCRIPTION: An editorial sky-blue sheet with cream specimen cards: each cloud species gets a CSS-only mini sky — absolutely-positioned white ellipses (with ::before/::after sub-puffs and a blur filter) composed per species: cauliflower cumulus, violent cumulonimbus stacks, combed cirrus filaments, a flat stratus lid, stacked lenticular wedding cakes, and hanging mammatus pouches squashed via elliptical border-radius. Cards carry altitude, type tag, and a dashed spec table; clicking one reveals italic field notes below with a scroll nudge and an orange rule.
TECHNIQUES: composition of pseudo-element puff clusters for procedurally-placed CSS cloud art, per-species layout data tables driving the DOM, elliptical border-radius tricks for lenticular and mammatus forms, gradient sky panels, card-selected state with detail panel injection.
INTERACTION: Click any specimen card for its field notes, hover for a lift, and use the guide outside immediately — cumulus humilis is almost certainly happening right now.
