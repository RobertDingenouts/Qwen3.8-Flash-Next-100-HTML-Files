TITLE: Hex Clock — the day rendered as one color
PROMPT: Tell time the way the web does: HHMMSS folded into #rrggbb, the hour bleeding into red, minutes into green, seconds into blue — and let the whole page slowly wear the mood of its own moment.
DESCRIPTION: A monospace terminal clock where each hex digit lives in its own glowing bordered tile that hops when it changes, tinted along a hue wheel from R to B. Below, three gradient meters fill red/green/blue channels to their true 24/60/60 ranges, a small oracle names the current colour in human (“sea teal”, “burnt amber”, “almost black”), and the body background drifts — a slow one-second crossfade into a deep, darkened impression of right now. Tap the big hex and it copies itself to your clipboard.
TECHNIQUES: time-to-rgb mapping with per-channel normalized meters, rgb→hsl conversion for perceptual naming and adaptive background tinting, per-digit change animation via transform hops, clipboard API with execCommand fallback, CSS transitions for the ambient colour drift.
INTERACTION: Watch the numbers and the mood-word; tap the hex readout to copy the current colour code; that’s all — it’s a clock, it does one thing perfectly.
