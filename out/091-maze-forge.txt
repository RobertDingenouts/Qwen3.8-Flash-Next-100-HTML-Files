TITLE: Maze Forge — inked with depth-first, solved with A*
PROMPT: Two algorithms on one sheet of graph paper: a maze carved by a drunk depth-first wanderer, then solved by A* with a visible frontier — watch the machine think in rust-red before it commits its answer in ink.
DESCRIPTION: Green graph paper holds a canvas maze whose walls are stroked in dark ink at line-weight matched to cell size. FORGE regenerates at any odd size 9–41 with recursive backtracker carving, keeping the perfect-maze property. SOLVE IT runs a genuine A* with Manhattan heuristic over the wall bitmask: the expanded frontier smoulders as translucent rust squares behind the search head, then the final path draws itself stroke-by-stroke from the emerald start dot to the vermilion goal. Press SOLVE IT again to wipe the ink and re-decide; the stat line reports path length honestly.
TECHNIQUES: recursive-backtracker maze generation over a 4-bit-per-cell wall mask, A* with g/f bookkeeping and wall-gated adjacency, live frontier windowing during search, incremental path reveal animation via interval stepping, ink-line wall rendering respecting outer boundaries.
INTERACTION: Slide SIZE and forge a fresh labyrinth, SOLVE IT to watch the frontier creep diagonally toward the goal and then commit its path, and solve the same maze again to clear the ink — the maze doesn’t remember, but the paper would like you to.
