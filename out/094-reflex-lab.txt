TITLE: Reflex Lab — wait for green, fail nobly
PROMPT: A stoplight on a chalkboard measuring the only honest number in human-computer interaction: your click latency, with a false-start counter to make sure you understand what waiting means.
DESCRIPTION: A fat lamp disc (radial highlight, inset bottom shadow) cycles red → random green (1.2–3.8s) → your click turns it verdict-blue with the exact milliseconds stamped inside it. Clicking during red is declared a FALSE START, increments the public shame counter, and the serif line below tells you the light hasn’t forgiven you. Twelve most recent attempts stand as green chalk bars under their own numbers — shorter is faster, the chart is honest, the best/avg pair update live — and each verdict band moves from “inhuman. were you a race car driver?” to a tortoise-related rebuke.
TECHNIQUES: three-state timing machine (arm/wait/go) with randomized setTimeout latency, performance.now millisecond resolution, rolling histogram of recent attempts with normalized bar heights, aggregate best/mean computation, stoplight colour theming with gradient lamp construction.
INTERACTION: Click the lamp to arm the trial, keep your finger honest during red, hit it instantly on green — and watch your last twelve attempts as a green skyline you can only improve by waiting properly.
