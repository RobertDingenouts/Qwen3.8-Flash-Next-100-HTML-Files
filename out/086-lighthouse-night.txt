TITLE: Lighthouse Night — rotation, fog, and one honest counter
PROMPT: A midnight sea toy with one job done properly: a rotating dual lighthouse beam whose real geometry decides which passing ship is sighted — fog thins the stars, waves bob the hulls, and the log counts only what the light actually caught.
DESCRIPTION: A cliff-hooked lighthouse with banded tower and glowing lamp room throws two opposite gradient light cones across a star-flecked sea; a banded moon dims with the fog slider. Ships — hulls with lit portholes and funnels — sail both ways, bobbing on a sine, and the moment a hull crosses within the beam’s angular wedge it flares, and SHIPS SIGHTED increments. Three drifting radial fog banks mute the sky, and wave lines undulate at different speeds. Rotation and fog are live sliders; the beam’s lamp pulses each sweep north.
TECHNIQUES: angular beam-sector hit-testing via atan2 and wrapped angle difference, gradient cone rendering as clipped pie wedges, particle-ish ship lifecycle with sine bob and porthole lighting, fog banks as travelling radial gradients, star twinkle with fog-aware alpha.
INTERACTION: Spin the rotation slider from glacial to helicopter, drown the scene in FOG until the moon disappears, and watch the counter in the corner keep the books — light caught a ship, or it didn’t.
