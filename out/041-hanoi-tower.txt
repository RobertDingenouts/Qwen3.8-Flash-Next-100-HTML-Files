TITLE: Tower of Hanoi — the monks are watching
PROMPT: Dress the oldest recursion joke in the business as a mossy garden puzzle: carved wooden rings in eight glazes, a granite plinth, move counting against the 2ⁿ−1 minimum, and a “monks’ way” button that animates the recursive solution ring by ring.
DESCRIPTION: A canvas tower garden with three mahogany pegs and a stone base; rings are gradient-glazed capsules with rim light. Grab-and-drop transfers are pointer-native (pick up a peg's top ring, ghost it under the cursor, drop it on a peg), wide-on-narrow placements earn a polite rebuke, and the header keeps a running tally against the theoretical minimum. The monks' way queues the recursive plan and plays it back at speed; completing on the third peg in the minimum number of moves triggers a wry toast about the heat death of the universe.
TECHNIQUES: state stacks per peg with legality guards, pointer drag with floating ghost ring, recursive move-queue generation for auto-solve with timed playback, responsive peg/ring geometry, gradient capsule rendering with rounded rects, live parity between moves and 2ⁿ−1.
INTERACTION: Drag the exposed ring of any peg onto another peg (or click to lift, click to set down), choose the ring count 3–8, SHOW THE MONKS’ WAY to watch the optimal recursive dance, BEGIN AGAIN to start the epoch over.
