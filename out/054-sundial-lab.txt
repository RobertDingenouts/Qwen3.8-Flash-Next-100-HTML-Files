TITLE: Sundial Lab — a stick, a shadow, a planet
PROMPT: Give the visitor an orrery-sized question in miniature — where would a stick’s shadow point, right now, at this latitude on this day — and answer it with honest spherical astronomy painted as an antique brass dial.
DESCRIPTION: A cream stone dial with a central gnomon, eight bearing lines, and hour ticks computed fresh from the current latitude and day of year: the shadow is drawn exactly where a real style would throw it from the sun’s solar azimuth and altitude, its length governed by cotangent of elevation. A golden sun with altitude-scaled glow rides the rim; winter latitudes after dusk flood the plate blue and admit defeat. Sliders for hour, day and latitude turn the earth under the stick; TELL ME NOW snaps to your actual clock and date.
TECHNIQUES: solar position via declination, hour angle, altitude/azimuth trigonometry (with quadrant fix for afternoon), inverse-tangent shadow geometry on a polar dial, pre-solved hour-tick loop skipping below-horizon hours, live HTML readout of altitude/azimuth/declination, responsive lab layout.
INTERACTION: Slide HOUR across the day and watch the shadow sweep the hour marks, drag DAY through the year to see seasons tilt the dial, flip LATITUDE past the poles to lose the sun entirely, and TELL ME NOW to find the real shadow of this moment.
