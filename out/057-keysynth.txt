TITLE: Key Synth — your keyboard is now an instrument
PROMPT: A one-screen synth rack that treats QWERTY like a keybed: two chromatic rows under your fingers, a live phosphor oscilloscope above, and a delay line that turns clumsy chords into ambient sketches.
DESCRIPTION: A tiny WebAudio polyphonic synth: twenty-nine keys drawn on canvas (lower rows Z–M and Q–P map to chromatic semitones) glow violet when held; each note is an oscillator voice with pluck envelope (attack → decay-to-sustain → exponential release) routed through master and an echo bus with feedback. The scope canvas renders the actual time-domain waveform from an AnalyserNode with a glowing green trace and fading persistence. Waveform, echo amount, octave and release sliders retune the machine live.
TECHNIQUES: WebAudio voice-per-note with scheduled gain envelopes and delayed stop, feedback delay bus with parallel wet gain, AnalyserNode float32 oscilloscope with translucent persistence, canvas keybed with press states and glow pass, keyboard event mapping to musical indices plus pointer keybing fallback.
INTERACTION: Hold Z S X D C V… for the lower octave, Q 2 W 3 E R… for the upper, stack keys for chords; click the drawn keys on touch; tweak WAVE for timbre, ECHO for space, OCTAVE to shift registers, RELEASE to choose pluck or pad.
